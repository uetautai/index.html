Mission Statement

Broken Stitch provides after-hours crisis support and a safe place to land for young people on youth justice orders in Logan. We respond when other services have closed for the night.

We focus on stabilising the immediate situation — de-escalation, safety, and connecting the young person and their family to the right supports.

Where longer-term or specialist help is needed, we refer to organisations that are set up to deliver that support during business hours.

ABOUT US

A stitch is meant to hold things together. When a stitch breaks, the fabric starts to pull apart.

We support young people who often come from families and environments Broken Stitch represents the moment when support failed — not the person. Many young people who reoffend aren’t broken. The systems, opportunities, trauma responses and guidance that were meant to hold them together were. The broken stitch symbolises the gaps in education, mentorship, stability and belonging that caused lives to unravel.

But a broken stitch can be repaired — often stronger than before. In tailoring, when a stitch breaks you don’t discard the garment. You restitch it, reinforcing it so it won’t fail the same way again.

Broken Stitch exists to re-stitch lives. We provide structure, skills, accountability and belief where there were once gaps. The logo’s broken stitch pattern reflects both disruption and intentional repair — a visible reminder that growth comes from fixing what was overlooked, not throwing it away.

Mission Statment

We are not the solution to the problems these young people face. Those problems run deeper than any single program can claim to fix.

As Thomas Sowell put it: there are no solutions — only trade-offs.

Broken Stitch offers practical trade-offs. We provide real alternatives that help meet the criminogenic needs that drive offending behaviour — structure, consistent presence, safe places to go, and people who will not walk away when the formal systems close for the night. We step into the gaps. That is the work.

Services

What we provide — and what we refer on

1

Crisis intervention

Immediate response when a young person is in crisis after hours — de-escalation and practical safety first.

2

After-hours support

Reachable when other services are closed. Pick-up, on-scene support, or a place to stay at the safe house when there is nowhere else to go.

3

Family engagement

Working with families where it is safe and appropriate, so the young person is not left to navigate the night alone.

4

Referral

We refer to professional organisations that can provide ongoing, specialist or daytime support. We do not deliver case management or long-term case work.

What we do not do

Broken Stitch does not provide case management, ongoing case support, or daytime program delivery. Our role is after-hours crisis response, safety, and connection to the services that are set up for the rest.
