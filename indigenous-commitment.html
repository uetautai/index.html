This Country

Before we speak of our work, we name this place, and the Elders and old people who belong to it.

The place

BrokenStitch works in Logan. This is not a place we simply operate in. It is home to families, communities and young people, and it has a history that began long before us.

We name this place: Logan, Queensland, along the river and the ridges.

We name the peoples whose old people belong here, where several Countries meet: Yugambeh, Yuggera (Yagara), Yugarapul, and Turrbal.

We pay our respects to Elders past and present. We name Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander families who live here, work here, raise children here, and keep carrying knowledge, culture and connection.

Who stands here

The old people come first. They hold the names, the manners, and the memory of this Country. They are not a problem. They are the standing of the place.

We honour those who have gone, and those who sit with us now.

Elders matter because young people matter. The people who came before us matter because the people who come after us matter.

How the young regard them

A living culture teaches a child where they stand by how they regard the elderly. You greet the old people first. You listen before you speak. You look for their faces in the room. You carry their names with care.

That regard is not pity. It is belonging. A child who knows how to honour the old people knows they come from somewhere, and that they are held.

We believe a young person should know where they come from, know who stands behind them, and know that their future is bigger than the circumstances they face today.

What we will not do

For us, naming Country is also about the young people. Some Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander young people in this community meet systems earlier, and more often, than they should. We do not look away from that.

We do not treat culture, identity, or where a young person comes from as the problem.

BrokenStitch comes here to stand beside young people — not to speak for communities, claim their culture, or pretend we have all the answers.

How we walk here

We come as a guest in the published window: Monday–Friday, 5:00 pm–10:00 pm. We do not claim this Country. We do not speak over the old people. We listen to families, kin, and Elders. We learn. We show up. We do not claim a promised result.

Where a young person needs someone to stand beside them, that is the work.
